# Hellobiotic visual-language dossier

**Research date:** 2026-08-31  
**Scope:** Public storefront, product presentation, public Instagram profile/grid, and Meta Ad Library query.  
**Status:** Observed public research. It is not an approved proprietary design system, a rights grant, or evidence of advertising performance.

## Executive read

Hellobiotic presents a small, intentionally soft personal-care world: a warm off-white field, dark charcoal typography, a single acidic yellow product cue, and a contrast between editorial restraint and playful microbiome language. The distinctive move is to make a technically framed product feel tactile and bodily rather than clinical. The visual system’s most consistent motifs are daylight, exposed skin, water, warm-weather ritual, organic texture, and a small family of hand-drawn emblematic marks.

The public evidence supports a **quiet sunlit-apothecary** direction—not a generic “clean beauty” system. The yellow cap/product mark is the sharp focal point; everything else makes room for it.

## The visual system

### 1. Color and material

- **Base field:** warm near-white / faint pink-grey (`rgb(241, 239, 238)` observed), not stark white. It makes pages feel skincare-editorial and gives product photography a gallery-like amount of air.
- **Signature accent:** saturated lemon yellow (`rgb(236, 217, 37)` observed on the product CTA). It appears in the deodorant cap, the thin claim ticker, pill-shaped CTAs, logo accents, and selected illustrated marks. It is the primary recognition asset.
- **Type and structure:** dark charcoal (`rgb(67, 65, 67)` observed), hairline rules, and occasional large charcoal panels. The contrast is gentle but deliberate rather than high-chroma or maximal.
- **Material cues:** stone, sand, straw/woven texture, water, citrus, botanical greenery, ice, fabric/towels, and warm sunlight. These ground microbiome science in touchable, outdoor, body-scale experience.

### 2. Typography and voice

- **Display:** `Cormorant` is the observed high-contrast editorial serif for headlines and product names. It creates an elevated, slightly romantic counterweight to the scientific category.
- **Utility:** `AktivGrotesk` is the observed sans serif for navigation, body copy, labels, claims, and e-commerce controls.
- **Hierarchy:** very large serif headlines sit in generous empty space; body copy is compact and centered on informational panels. The product PDP pairs the large serif name with conventional commerce details.
- **Voice:** the copy moves between playful/body-positive language—“pits,” “everyBODY,” “pit stop,” “Don’t Sweat It”—and product-system language—“Full-Spectrum Microbiome Care,” pre/pro/postbiotics, ingredient roles. The tone is friendly, unisex, and lightly irreverent, not medical.

### 3. Graphic devices

- A narrow, persistent **yellow claim ticker** carries short repeated credentials such as microbiome-friendly, natural, no aluminum, no baking soda, and no synthetic fragrance. It works as a proof ribbon, not a hero message.
- The logo is a refined lowercase **biotic** wordmark with small yellow star/sun-like accents. A compact `b.` mark appears in footer contexts.
- The site uses a family of **thin hand-drawn benefit icons**: balancing, pH, body friendliness, odor-causing bacteria, baking-soda-free, aluminum-free, natural, paraben-free, cruelty-free, and made-with-love. Yellow selectively punctuates the group.
- The “What is microbiome-friendly?” explanation uses a loose, starburst/celestial collage rather than scientific diagrams. This avoids laboratory literalism and reinforces a playful, magical reading of “biotic.”

### 4. Image direction

**Product photography:**

- White cylindrical packaging with an unmistakable yellow cap is treated as the hero object.
- The product is often photographed upright, with ample negative space and simple prop worlds: raw stone, woven material, ice, towels, botanicals, or a hand.
- The pack is legible at small scale. The yellow cap remains the compositional anchor even when a human scene is present.

**Lifestyle and social imagery:**

- Public Instagram content is broader and more documentary than the storefront: bodies in sun/shade, swimming water, beach reading, towels, ice, pool architecture, flowers, citrus, and close product-in-hand shots.
- The current visible grid pairs product still-life and product-in-use imagery with wellness/seasonal environment shots. Several posts use short overlaid editorial text, such as reminders or summer-heat framing.
- People are portrayed as relaxed, ordinary, and unisex; the feed does not read as heavily retouched prestige beauty or clinical dermatology.

## Surface-specific observations

### Storefront

The homepage uses oversized white space and sparse centered sections. The hero places a serif headline to the left of a product still-life on stone. Subsequent sections alternate between central explanatory text and small graphic/icon moments. The experience intentionally slows the shopper down: science/claims are distributed through the page rather than delivered in a dense performance-marketing stack. [Storefront](https://www.hellobiotic.com/)

### Product detail page

The deodorant page follows the same off-white/charcoal/yellow system. A large pack image is allowed to dominate the left field, while the title, reviews, price, explanatory copy, and purchase controls sit to the right. The design treats the product label as a visible asset, not merely a pack shot. [Deodorant PDP](https://www.hellobiotic.com/products/brightening-probiotic-deodorant-1)

The product page’s public language frames the product around “microbiome-friendly” deodorant and a proprietary “Biome Balancing Blend.” Those are brand-authored product claims, not independent efficacy findings. [Deodorant PDP](https://www.hellobiotic.com/products/brightening-probiotic-deodorant-1)

### Instagram

The public profile uses the round `b.`/yellow-on-light identity and presents a bio around probiotic brightening unisex deodorant, naturalness, and balancing the microbiome. Its highlights visibly cover retailer/founder/product/reading/seasonal moments. The visible grid is coherent with the site, but less controlled: it uses sunshine, water, heat, bodies, and everyday lifestyle scenes to make the product social and seasonal.

Do not infer audience makeup, engagement, performance, ownership of third-party photography, or the complete posting strategy from this accessible surface. [@hellobiotic](https://www.instagram.com/hellobiotic/)

## Working creative principles (inferred; not brand rules)

1. Let the **yellow cap/mark** do the recognition work; keep supporting fields quiet.
2. Use editorial serif scale and generous air, then ground it with compact sans-serif utility.
3. Treat microbiome language as a bridge to everyday embodied ritual—sun, water, softness, warmth, motion—not a laboratory aesthetic.
4. Pair an object with a tactile context; do not use sterile floating-pack mockups as the primary image language.
5. Use body imagery with ease and inclusivity; avoid shame, odor panic, exaggerated transformation, or gendered deodorant cliches.
6. Preserve a meaningful distinction between **observed style** and **health/product claims**. Any new efficacy, safety, or medical language requires appropriate substantiation and approval.
