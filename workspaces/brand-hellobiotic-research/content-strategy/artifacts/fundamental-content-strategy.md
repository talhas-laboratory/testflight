# Fundamental content strategy

Version 0.1 · candidate foundation · 2026-09-08

## The thesis

The most relevant brand content does not begin with the product. It begins with the field in which
a person is already trying to understand, become, decide, or do something.

A product is often one instrument inside that field. A brand is one participant with a particular
point of view and degree of earned credibility. The person is not reducible to a “customer”: they
have practices, tensions, curiosity, identity, and a changing relationship to the field. A useful
newsletter finds the live common ground among those relations and contributes something of value
there.

This makes relational fit—not product proximity—the primary unit of content relevance.

## The editorial promise

Biotic helps readers understand and navigate the living systems, ingredients, choices, and daily
practices around skin and hygiene with curiosity, evidence, and practical care.

Every issue should leave the reader with at least one of the following:

- a clearer model of something they care about;
- a useful distinction that changes how they judge a claim or choice;
- a safe, proportionate practice they can try;
- a question worth carrying forward;
- a more capable and less anxious relationship with their body or routine.

The product may appear when it genuinely helps explain or enact the subject. It is not owed a role
in every issue.

## The relational field

The working composition is:

```text
person ↔ self-concept / desired way of being
   ↕
field of engagement ↔ tensions, questions, practices, communities, knowledge
   ↕                         ↕
brand ↔ point of view     product ↔ instrument / experience
   ↘                         ↙
       content contribution
              ↕
        evidence and learning
```

The field is not a demographic category. It is a changing domain of attention and action: for
example vegan skincare, skin ecology, everyday hygiene, or self-image. A person can participate in
several fields at once and with different motivations. Personalization should therefore select a
supported relational composition for the moment, not assign a permanent identity to the person.

## How content earns relevance

A topic is promising when it satisfies five conditions:

1. **Field centrality:** it belongs to a field the reader is demonstrably engaging with.
2. **Lived stake:** it connects to a real question, tension, practice, choice, or desired change.
3. **Contribution:** it offers insight, utility, perspective, or constructive surprise.
4. **Legitimate voice:** Biotic has an honest reason to participate and does not pretend to possess
   authority it lacks.
5. **Reader independence:** the piece remains worth reading even if the reader never buys the
   product.

Product relevance is secondary and can strengthen a piece only when it is natural, non-coercive,
and useful.

## Initial Biotic content territories

### Hygiene systems and trade-offs

Investigate conventions, ingredients, routines, marketing claims, and unintended effects. Avoid
the blanket premise that “mainstream hygiene is harmful.” Ask which practice or ingredient may
cause which effect, for whom, under what conditions, and according to what evidence.

### Ingredients, ethics, and the body

Explain ingredients at the level where a mechanism can be supported. Keep ethical and sourcing
properties—vegan, plant-derived, cruelty-free—distinct from biological effects. Explore where
these concerns meet and where they do not.

### Skin ecology and microbiomes

Make biomes, microbiomes, bacteria, skin barriers, odor, and bodily systems intelligible without
turning emerging science into certainty. Prefer mental models, competing explanations, and
evidence limits over miracle narratives.

### Everyday practices

Offer small, accessible hygiene practices and experiments. State prerequisites, risks, expected
effort, and when professional advice is appropriate. A “hack” should reduce friction without
trading away safety or truthfulness.

### Self-image and embodied life

Treat self-image as a possible wider field, not an assumed vulnerability to exploit. Content may
explore norms, comfort, shame, confidence, care, and bodily autonomy when there is evidence that a
reader or audience wants that conversation. The aim is agency, not insecurity-driven conversion.

## The issue pattern

A strong issue normally contains:

1. a recognizable reader question or scene;
2. a tension that is specific enough to investigate;
3. a deep explanation with visible evidence and uncertainty;
4. a practical implication or experiment;
5. an optional connection to Biotic's point of view or product, only if earned;
6. a closing question or feedback prompt that improves the relational model.

Depth does not mean length. It means resolving a useful distinction instead of repeating familiar
advice.

## Personalization without reduction

Customization should use the minimum supported context necessary: current field, active question,
experience level, practical constraints, and desired depth. It should not infer health status,
identity, insecurity, or motive from purchase behavior alone.

When evidence is weak, generate a broad issue or ask a respectful preference question. Preserve
multiple plausible compositions rather than silently choosing the most commercially convenient
one.

## Evidence and editorial boundaries

- Strategic direction is not customer evidence.
- Audience hypotheses are not individual facts.
- Retrieved sources are evidence inputs, not automatic conclusions.
- Scientific, health, safety, and efficacy claims require traceable sources and proportionate
  review.
- “Natural,” “vegan,” “microbiome-friendly,” and similar terms must not smuggle in unsupported
  claims.
- The agent proposes; an authorized human or workflow approves publication.

## How this manifest evolves

Revisions should preserve the source of a change and classify it as founder direction, audience
evidence, scientific evidence, editorial learning, or observed performance. Metrics can reveal
response but cannot alone define value. Open rate and clicks should be read alongside trust,
usefulness, correction burden, unsubscribes, qualitative feedback, and the diversity of fields
served.

The central research program is to learn which relational compositions produce genuinely useful
content, why they work, and how much context is necessary to recognize them without reducing the
reader to a segment.

## Open questions

- What are Biotic's distinct fields of legitimate participation, and where are their boundaries?
- Which reader tensions are observed, and which are still founder hypotheses?
- What signals reveal a person's current field without invasive inference?
- How should relation strength, timing, and contradictions affect topic selection?
- What forms of depth work for novices, practitioners, and highly informed readers?
- How do we measure durable usefulness rather than momentary attention?
- When does a product connection enrich an issue, and when does it diminish trust?
