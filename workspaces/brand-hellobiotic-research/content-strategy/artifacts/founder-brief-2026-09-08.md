# Founder brief: Biotic newsletter and relational content

## Source status

- Captured: 2026-09-08
- Authority: founder-authored strategic direction supplied in the working conversation
- Epistemic status: intended direction and conceptual hypothesis
- Permitted use: develop and evaluate Biotic content strategy and newsletter-agent proposals
- Not evidence of: customer beliefs, customer behavior, topic performance, or scientific truth

## Intent

Create a newsletter agent for Biotic whose first obligation is to produce material that readers
actually find relevant, helpful, or interesting. The newsletter does not need to discuss the
product directly. Its natural subject is the common ground between customer, brand, and product.

People generally use a product within a much larger field of engagement. A person's use of a
product may be one expression of a broader concern, practice, interest, or self-understanding. The
brand and product each occupy a position in that field; neither should be treated as its center by
default.

For Biotic, one candidate field is vegan skincare and hygiene. Other possible fields include skin
care, the bodily microbiome, everyday hygiene practice, and self-image. These are hypotheses to be
researched and differentiated rather than collapsed into a single audience identity.

## Initial content territories

- Investigations of where mainstream hygiene ingredients or practices may create problems, with
  claims narrowed by ingredient, exposure, population, and quality of evidence.
- Ingredient-level explanations of how plant-derived or non-animal components interact with the
  body; “vegan” itself is a sourcing and ethical property, not a biological mechanism.
- Deep dives into biomes, microbiomes, bacteria, skin, and their relationships with the body.
- Low-friction hygiene practices, tips, and experiments that are safe, practical, and honest about
  their limits.

## Long-term ambition

Formalize the relational composition among person, self, field of engagement, brand, product,
practice, and evidence. Use that composition to select and write content for people rather than to
push product-adjacent topics. Refine the model over time using explicit audience evidence,
editorial review, and observed response.
