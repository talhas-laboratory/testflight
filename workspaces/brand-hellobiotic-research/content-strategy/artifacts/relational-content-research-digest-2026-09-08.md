# Relational-content research digest: five design questions

Status: research-informed proposal. This digest does not establish anything about Biotic readers.
It links external findings to the founder-directed relational-content model and records where the
model must remain uncertain.

## 1. Is a field of engagement more than an interest, identity, or segment?

**Finding.** Ethical consumption research connects values, identity, routines, social context, and
market choices, but also documents a material gap between ethical self-description and purchase
behaviour. Identity may therefore be a component of engagement, not its synonym or a reliable
targeting signal. [Kossmann & Gomez-Suarez, 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31849794/)
and [Werle et al., 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33796041/) support this distinction.

**Concept effect.** Retain `field_of_engagement` as a changing context of practice, concern,
learning, and identity. Do not collapse it into a segment label, stated value, or purchase event.

## 2. How can the agent infer a field without over-personalising?

**Finding.** Identity measures are often shallow and do not capture shifting or multiple identities.
[Marshall et al., 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36079713/) This supports a conservative
inference posture. The ICO also treats profiling/inference accuracy and data minimisation as
material concerns. [ICO AI guidance](https://ico.org.uk/media2/ga4lfb5d/guidance-on-ai-and-data-protection-all-2-0-38.pdf)

**Agent rule proposal.** Use explicit preferences, volunteered questions, and bounded observed
feedback as evidence. Otherwise select a broad, non-personalised issue or return `ask`; never
infer health, sensitive identity, insecurity, or motive from purchasing or engagement metrics.

## 3. What contribution deepens a field instead of merely filling a channel?

**Finding.** Research on ethical living suggests ethics can be expressed through ordinary care and
routines rather than only explicit political consumer choice. [Hawkins & colleagues, 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28556919/)
Research on co-creation also indicates that interaction can create value, though effects vary by
context. [Wang et al., 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39767318/)

**Concept effect.** Prioritise contributions that clarify a question, improve a practice, expose a
trade-off, or invite non-extractive reflection. Product adjacency alone is insufficient.

## 4. How can self-image enter without exploiting insecurity?

**Finding.** A systematic review of 95 studies associates idealised advertising with harmful
self-perception outcomes; evidence for non-idealised imagery is mixed rather than automatically
protective. [Frontiers review, 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39845552/) [systematic review,
2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37871529/)

**Agent rule proposal.** Self-image can be a voluntary, reflective branch—not a conversion lever.
Reject content that manufactures deficiency, compares bodies, promises transformation, or treats a
disclaimer as sufficient protection. Use care, agency, and practical context instead.

## 5. When does Biotic have legitimate standing to speak?

**Finding.** Health- and safety-related claims require appropriate substantiation; implied claims
matter alongside explicit ones. [FTC Health Products Compliance Guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/health-products-compliance-guidance)

**Concept effect.** Brand legitimacy is not topic ownership. Biotic may frame questions, explain
evidence limits, and offer bounded practical relevance. It may not imply product efficacy or a
biological mechanism from “vegan,” which is a sourcing/ethical attribute.

## Theory update

The field-first branch remains useful, with a refinement:

```text
explicit or responsibly bounded context
  -> field / question / practice hypothesis
  -> reader-independent contribution
  -> evidence and legitimacy gate
  -> optional brand or product relation
```

`self_concept` is now an optional, high-care branch. It requires explicit reader evidence or a
non-personalised editorial treatment. The next empirical need is qualitative reader research,
not further inference from brand data.

## Sources and limits

The cited studies concern adjacent domains—ethical consumption, eating, sustainability, media,
and advertising—not Biotic subscribers or vegan skincare specifically. Their use here is to bound
the theory and agent behaviour, not to predict audience response.
